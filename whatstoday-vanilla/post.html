Why a weak won isn't automatically a win for Korean exporters in 2026

I work in supply-chain for a mid-cap electronics firm. The textbook logic of "weak won equals export boom" breaks down once you factor in how dependent we've become on imported US chips, energy, and intermediate goods. Here's what we're actually seeing on the ground.

First, hedging matters. The major chaebols have been running 80-90% FX hedge programs since January. The benefit of a weak won shows up in their headline numbers, but their operating margin is largely neutralized by the hedges already in place. The smaller manufacturers — the ones who actually need pricing flexibility — usually can't afford forward contracts at this scale.

Chart: USD/KRW vs export volume, 2020–2026

Second, our import bill is structural. Energy alone is 30% of total imports. A weaker won makes every kilowatt-hour more expensive — and that cost gets passed downstream into our own production costs, partially erasing whatever margin the weak won was supposed to bring back.
